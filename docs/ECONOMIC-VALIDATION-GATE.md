# ECONOMIC VALIDATION GATE — Creative Ops Autopilot
Date: 2026-10-01
Status: ACTIVE
Principle: prove willingness-to-pay and delivery economics before new fixed infrastructure spend.

## Decision
Do NOT buy Railway or paid video subscriptions yet.

## Controlled founder-funded proof budget
Hard cap before first paid client: **US$30 total**.
Use only if a specific prospect package cannot be made credible with free/current resources.
Every expense must have:
- prospect name
- asset/job purpose
- expected learning
- max spend
- stop-loss
- result

No recurring subscription is authorized under this gate.

## Pre-contact spec, free to the prospect
Build privately from public data:
- one buyer-specific problem diagnosis
- one high-impact creative concept
- three hooks
- one storyboard / shot plan
- one policy/claim warning
- one test hypothesis
This is the door-opener, not the full paid delivery.

## Founding paid sprint
Do NOT anchor the service at US$149 if execution includes finished creative production.
Recommended first paid range after measuring delivery cost: **US$299–499** for a scoped sprint.
Final price is set after measuring:
1. research time
2. strategy time
3. asset-generation cash cost
4. editing/render time
5. QA/revision time
6. expected support burden

Long-term retainers must be priced only after 1–3 paid deliveries reveal real unit economics.

## Unit economics
For each job record:
- cash_received
- taxes_and_fees
- provider_cost
- infra_cost_allocated
- human_hours
- rework_hours
- gross_margin_cash
- gross_margin_after_owner_time
- time_to_cash
- repeat_purchase
- attributed_result_if_available

A job is NOT economically validated merely because revenue > provider cost.

## Scale gates
Gate A — 1 paid client:
prove willingness-to-pay + delivery cost.

Gate B — 3 paid clients:
prove repeatable scope and price.

Gate C — 10 active clients:
prove operating cadence, QA, support and cost routing.

Gate D — 100 active clients:
requires automated queue, asset storage, billing, observability, policy profiles, provider routing, human escalation.

Gate E — 1,000 active clients:
requires self-service onboarding, quotas, usage accounting, redundancy, provider failover, country/channel policy versioning, audit logs, support operations.

Gate F — 10,000 active clients:
treat as SaaS infrastructure, not agency workflow. Requires formal SRE/security/compliance/customer-success architecture.

## Our moat cannot be "AI video generation"
Current competitors already offer AI ad generation, avatars, campaign launchers, creative insights, competitor tracking and performance agents.
Our differentiation must be:
- outside-in spec before sale
- Director that chooses what to make and why
- Product Fidelity Lock
- Policy-by-country/channel
- Cost Router
- Winner genealogy / controlled iterations
- Commercial Readiness Gate
- Learning Loop tied to next action
- done-for-you outcome, not tool training
- universal tenant engine across products/markets

## Stop-loss rules
- No paid infrastructure before a paid pilot or explicit owner override.
- No recurring paid provider until variable production demand exists.
- No offer that promises performance outcome we cannot attribute.
- No scaling price until real delivery cost is measured.
- No hard-coded Beco 13 architecture. Beco 13 remains Case Zero only.
