Hypothesis:
A mature-skin identity hook may outperform generic anti-aging language because the public job post explicitly says the brand is focused primarily on women 45+.

Hook variations:
1. "Your skin changed. Your routine should too."
2. "Skincare for 45+ should not feel like an afterthought."
3. "Stop talking to mature skin like it is twenty-five."

Creative beats:
0–2s: woman 45+ direct-to-camera, emotionally specific tension
2–5s: product appears immediately
5–12s: short routine demonstration

18–24s: brand-approved proof/review
24–30s: CTA + guarantee/offer only if currently valid

Test variable:
Identity-specific framing vs generic anti-aging framing.

### H2: Mechanism before miracle
Hypothesis:
Evidence-first creative may reduce skepticism in a category saturated with exaggerated anti-aging claims.

Hook variations:
1. "Before the promise, show me what the mask actually does."
2. "No miracle language. Show the mechanism."
3. "The tightening feeling is the story."

Creative beats:
0–3s: tactile peel/application macro
3–8s: simple explanation of physical routine/experience
8–15s: ingredient/mechanism only from brand-approved source
15–22s: verified review/proof
22–30s: CTA

Test variable:
Mechanism-first vs aspiration-first.

### H3: Overnight ritual / satisfying transformation
Hypothesis:
The peel/application ritual itself is visually satisfying and can carry retention before any claim appears.

Hook variations:
1. "The skincare step you actually look forward to peeling off."
2. "Your night routine does not need twelve steps."
3. "Put it on tonight. Peel the ritual off tomorrow."

Creative beats:
0–2s: peel/reveal visual hook
2–7s: application
7–14s: bedtime/overnight context
14–21s: next-morning sensory result language kept non-clinical unless approved
21–30s: offer/guarantee CTA

Test variable:
Sensory/ritual retention vs claim-led ad.
