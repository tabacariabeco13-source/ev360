# PREMIUM WELLNESS MINI-TEST — Portfolio Spec
Purpose: ready sample for buyers asking for hooks + scripts + testing plan.
Product: hypothetical premium heated eye mask.
Important: SPEC ONLY. No efficacy, medical or therapeutic claim is asserted.

## Customer tension
Busy professional wants a short evening ritual that feels premium and easy. The product is sold as an experience/convenience object, not as treatment.

## 10 hooks
1. “Ten quiet minutes before the rest of the house asks for you again.”
2. “My night routine got shorter when I stopped turning it into a project.”
3. “The product lives on my nightstand because I actually use it.”
4. “This is what ‘do nothing for ten minutes’ looks like.”
5. “I wanted a ritual, not another complicated routine.”
6. “The easiest premium upgrade in my evening takes one button.”
7. “No twelve-step routine. Just heat, darkness and ten minutes.”
8. “The best part of my evening routine is the part with no screen.”
9. “If your self-care needs a spreadsheet, it is probably too complicated.”
10. “A small object that makes the room feel like the day is finally over.”

## Script 1 — Creator native / routine
Length: 25–30s
0–2s: creator drops phone face-down. Hook on screen: “The best part of my routine has no screen.”
2–6s: product appears immediately. Creator puts it on.
6–13s: show one-button setup, fit and premium materials.
13–20s: quiet lifestyle shots: couch, bedside, dim room. VO focuses on convenience and ritual, not health outcomes.
20–26s: product close-up and removal.
26–30s: CTA: “See how it fits into your evening routine.”

Test variable: hook only. Keep body, visual order and CTA constant across first three cuts.

## Script 2 — Anti-complexity / problem-solution
Length: 25–30s
0–3s: cluttered vanity / too many products. Hook: “If self-care needs a spreadsheet, I’m out.”
3–7s: cut to a single premium eye mask.
7–14s: simple setup demonstration.
14–21s: tactile product details + relaxed environment.
21–27s: “One simple step I can actually repeat.”
27–30s: CTA: “Make the routine easier.”

Test variable: first 3 seconds. Version A uses clutter contrast. Version B opens on tactile product macro. Version C opens with creator direct-to-camera.

## Testing plan
Phase 1: Hook test
- same body, same offer, same CTA
- 3 hook variants
- judge early attention using hook/hold metrics and downstream CTR

Phase 2: Angle test
- winner hook
- compare “screen-free ritual” vs “anti-complexity” vs “premium tactile experience”

Phase 3: Proof/offer
- only after angle winner emerges
- introduce verified customer language or offer variation

Learning rule:
Do not call a concept dead because one hook lost. Separate hook failure from body/angle failure.
